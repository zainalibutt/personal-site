# About copy — drafts

**These are drafts, not copy.** None of this is in Zain's voice yet; it exists so
there is something to react to instead of a blank request. Pick one, cut it
apart, or reject all three — the reaction is the useful output.

The About block is the most-read text on the page and the only prose on the
entry screen. It sits in the centre spine, roughly 380px wide, so each paragraph
wants to be two or three lines. Three paragraphs is the ceiling before the
column outgrows the artefacts flanking it.

## What every draft assumes

Two claims are carried over from the current placeholder and **need confirming**
before any of this ships:

- "Recently out of university" — is graduating the framing you want leading, or
  does it undersell?
- "already shipping for clients" — is there freelance work you can name, or does
  this stay abstract?

---

## Draft A — plain

Closest to what is on the page now, tightened. Says what you do, what you are
interested in, and what you want. No thesis, no flourish.

> I build product end to end — mobile, web, and the backend underneath.
>
> Recent work: a tool that proves a file has not been altered since it was
> captured, a keyboard-first terminal for reading company filings from primary
> sources, and a ledger that settles group expenses without a spreadsheet.
>
> Based in London. Open to roles and freelance work.

**Where it wins:** legible in four seconds, which is all a first visit gets.
**Where it loses:** could describe a lot of people. Nothing here is only you.

---

## Draft B — thesis

Leads with the question the projects have in common and lets the work argue for
it. Highest ceiling and the highest risk of sounding like a manifesto.

> Most of what I build circles one question: how do you know a record is true?
>
> Cryptographic provenance for media. Company filings pulled from the source
> rather than a summarised feed. A ledger that reconciles itself. The interesting
> part is never the claim — it is what you have to keep in order to check it
> later.
>
> Recently out of university, already shipping for clients. Based in London, open
> to roles and freelance work.

**Where it wins:** a stranger leaves with a sentence they could repeat about you,
and the projects below stop reading as unrelated.
**Where it loses:** if the thesis is retrofitted rather than true, a technical
reader will feel it. Only use this if it is how you actually pick what to build.

---

## Draft C — concrete

The register a hiring engineer reads fastest: specific nouns, no adjectives.

> TypeScript across the stack — React and Next on the front, Node and Postgres
> behind it, Expo when it needs to be on a phone.
>
> I like problems where the data fights back: normalising SEC filings that tag
> the same concept three different ways, signing a capture on-device so it can be
> verified by someone who does not trust me, keeping a timeline usable offline.
>
> London. Open to roles and freelance work.

**Where it wins:** every clause is checkable against the repositories below.
**Where it loses:** no warmth, and it says nothing about how you work with people.

---

## The question I cannot answer for you

Does the About block need to say what you are looking for more precisely than
"roles and freelance work"? Right now it commits to nothing — a reader who could
hire you does not know whether that means a graduate role, a contract, or a
first engineer seat. That specificity is worth more than any of the wording
above.
